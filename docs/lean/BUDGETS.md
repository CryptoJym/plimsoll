## 1. Host classes

Classes describe capture volume, not hardware. A collector classifies its host from the rolling 7-day **attempted** rows per day (admitted rows plus rows counted into declared gaps), re-evaluated daily; a throttled host therefore keeps its class. **A host with unread session files is unclassified** until its catch-up is complete (`bytesDeferred = 0`, a complete whole-root walk; ARCHITECTURE.md §6.4): Studio1 has 5.0 GB of parseable unread files against a 1.34 GB ledger, Studio4 1.3 GB against 1.36 GB (`UNCOVERED-EVIDENCE.txt:16-40`; `out/checks/plan-arithmetic.log`), so their attempted rates and ledger sizes today understate them by an unknown factor. Thresholds are initial values that the telemetry bead (B1) sets from real per-host rates, which are not in the inputs (the cloud saw ~400k rows/day by the one-hour extrapolation, `PROD-FACTS.md:29`; the per-machine split is unmeasured).

## 4. How the collector enforces the budgets

### 4.1 Two regimes: advisory until migrated, enforced after

- **Pre-migration (S0 through S9 on that host)**: every measure is sampled and shown, with the class target beside it, and nothing is enforced. The `/status` block says `budget.mode = "advisory"` and names the stage. No reduction step runs, no backpressure, no capture stop. The only hard stops in this regime are the conversion throttle's free-disk floor (§4.2), which stops conversion, not capture; the no-delete hold (ARCHITECTURE.md §2.4), which stops deletion; and the hold's abort ladder (§4.2), which releases acknowledged rows or aborts the migration when the runway runs out, never capture evidence.
- **Post-migration (after S10 on that host)**: the host's targets are **calibrated** from its own rehearsal or S10 measurement (converted size after rebuild, 24 h replay p95 of RSS, CPU and write rate) and then enforced: `budget.mode = "enforced"` with the calibration receipt. Calibration can only set a cap at or above the class figure in §2 when the measured need is higher, never below the measured steady state plus 25% headroom, and never by deleting evidence (`footprint-budgets.md:31`).

### 4.2 Conversion throttle, the hold runway and the abort ladder (independent of the caps; blocker 5)

The converter (MIGRATION.md S3) pauses when the 1-minute collector CPU exceeds 0.5 core on a light host, 1.0 on a busy host or 2.0 on Studio0 scale; when WAL bytes written exceed the class write figure for 5 consecutive minutes; when free disk on the volume falls under the reserve; or when the runway falls under 10 days. It never pauses on ledger size. It resumes when the measure has been under the threshold for 2 minutes. Every pause and its reason is in `/status` and the collector log.

**The runway replaces the one-time headroom gate.** Round 3 gated S2 on `free disk ≥ 1.25 × G + 2 × WAL_24h + live × 1.2`, a one-time check with no capture-growth term, while the hold stops every deletion for the whole migration (an estimated three to six weeks per host) and capture keeps appending (review R11). Round 4 (ARCHITECTURE.md §2.4) requires, before the hold is set on a host and continuously while it is on:

- `hold_growth_per_day = p95_7d(gross ledger growth per day) × (1 + g_host × raw_share)`, where gross growth is the daily increase of `(page_count − freelist_count) × page_size` plus the bytes today's prune deleted that day (its receipts × the host's measured bytes per raw row), `raw_share` is the raw table's share of the ledger (0.46-0.58 on the four measured hosts) and `g_host = G_gate / raw bytes` is the **same** conservative figure the numerator subtracts (round 7, `CONTRACTS.md` C2; the round-4 0.42 is retired) until S2 measures it on that host; the catch-up must be complete inside the seven-day window;
- `reserve = max(5% of the volume, 2 × WAL_24h high-water, 2 GiB)` (hypothesis; B1 calibrates it from the observed WAL peaks); `G_gate = 1.25 × G_host` from B1's counted census (§3, §4.3), or the copy's measured peak where one exists; `G = G_owed = max(0, G_gate − C_conv) + g_gate × U` while conversion runs (round 8, `CONTRACTS.md` C2: `C_conv` the converter's own written bytes, `U` the raw bytes admitted since the census that no lean row covers, `g_gate = G_gate / raw bytes at the census`; the lean tables' page count is not an input because it holds the live writer's rows), 0 once every census-era and post-census row is folded; `rebuild_headroom = live bytes × 1.2` (the rebuild's own need, MIGRATION.md §4);
- `runway_days = (free_disk − reserve − G − rebuild_headroom) / hold_growth_per_day`, recomputed every 60 s by B1 from the current free space and the trailing 7-day p95, published in `/status` and the collector log;
- **start rule**: the hold may be set only when `runway_days ≥ max(30, 1.5 × planned hold days)`;
- **ladder** while the hold is on, each rung disclosed in `/status` with its timestamp: `< 10 days` the converter pauses; `< 5 days` the hold is partially released and the prune resumes under the strict acknowledged-only predicate of ARCHITECTURE.md §2.4 (never a pending, dead, unmarked or withheld row, never a member of an open target; `out/checks/counterexamples.log`, case 12); `< 2 days` the host aborts the migration (`lean.write = off`, drop the new tables, rebuild, back to S0 with the reason recorded). Nothing unacknowledged is deleted at any rung, so capture evidence is preserved.

`out/checks/counterexamples.log`, case 15, and `plan-arithmetic.log` show a host that passes the one-time gate and fails the runway. The S10 free-space test comes too late for this risk and is not the gate.

### 4.3 Measurement (B1)

Every 60 s the collector records: DB + WAL + SHM bytes (`stat`), `page_count` and `freelist_count`, RSS of the supervisor and of the worker separately, CPU time deltas of both, WAL bytes written since the last sample (and the 24 h high-water), free bytes on the ledger's volume, outbox pending and dead ages, summary lag, per-source `bytesDeferred` and `deferredGenerations`, the last whole-root walk's completion time and coverage, the unread-file classes, open faults (ARCHITECTURE.md §6.3-6.4), and per-table page counts once a day from `dbstat` on light hosts only (never a heavy scan on Studio0: `HOSTS.md:19`). Once a day it derives **gross ledger growth** (page growth plus the bytes of the rows the prune deleted, from `raw_retention_receipts` written that day, `collector-cli/src/buffer.ts:3035-3039`), the activity-to-usage row ratio, the converter's written-bytes counter and the raw bytes admitted since the census that no lean row covers (`converter_written_bytes`, `raw_unfolded_bytes`, for `G_owed`; the lean tables' page count is disclosure only, round 8), and, once seven days exist, `hold_growth_per_day` and `runway_days` (§4.2). **Per light host, after the catch-up and at most a day before S1b, then again before S3,** B1 runs the cardinality census (`fixtures/host_cardinality_census.py`: usage and raw rows, sessions, session-day rows, day rows, rollup buckets, day targets, the segment proxy, the raw table's bytes and the timestamp shape classes) on a `VACUUM INTO` copy of the ledger, made only when free space is at least the ledger's bytes plus the reserve and deleted after the count, never on the live file and never on Studio0, and stores it with its timestamp as the basis of `G_host` (rounds 7-8, `CONTRACTS.md` C2; a stale census, one taken before the catch-up, or one without room for its copy gives no gate value). The last 24 h of samples live in a small ring table and in `/status`; the daily derivations are kept for 90 days. The RSS remedy (worker checkpoint and restart) targets the worker; if B1 shows the supervisor holds the RSS, that is a separate finding and the cap is not enforced against it until the cause is known.

### 4.4 Thresholds, reduction order, the physical reserve and a full volume (enforced regime only; should-fix 6)

At 75% of a cap: warn in `/status` and the local dashboard and schedule compaction. At 90%: run the reduction order below to the next step. At 100%: backpressure with disclosure. Hysteresis: a step is undone only when the measure has been under 60% for one hour. Each step is taken only if the previous one did not bring the ledger under 85%; each is disclosed with its timestamp.

1. Shorten the raw replay window R for proved, summarized activity rows: 72 h → 24 h → 6 h (floor). Deletion still goes through the proof (ARCHITECTURE.md §2.2).
2. Compact acknowledged upload receipts whose raw rows are gone into batch receipts (dead receipts untouched; B14, only once its proof exists).
3. Expire resolved repo-context links whose raw row is gone and metric samples older than 24 h.
4. Shorten R for proved usage rows the same way (their facts remain).
5. Drop `turn_summaries` older than 7 days. The session-level aggregates that M6 and M8 read (`tool_class_counts_json`, `spend_after_failure_by_class_json`, `spend_after_failure_by_class_model_json`, `context_thresholds_by_model_json`) are computed at capture on the session row (ARCHITECTURE.md §3.3), so nothing a manager metric reads is lost; only the per-session drill-down shortens.
6. Shorten local usage-fact retention toward the floor: the cloud-acknowledged horizon plus R, never below 7 days, never below the finance publication's period (ARCHITECTURE.md §2.1).
7. Run a maintenance rebuild (MIGRATION.md §4) if free pages exceed 20% of the file and disk headroom allows.

**Never removed by pressure:** the never-delete set in ARCHITECTURE.md §2.3, tombstones (until B14), and the day-grain dashboard history (a few MB).

**At 100% ledger, and where the reserve physically sits.** Let C be the enforced ledger cap. Above C the collector stops admitting new activity rows and counts them into a `capture_gap` (reason `footprint_cap`, `count_basis = 'counted'`) per source with the interval. Usage records keep being admitted into a **usage reserve band** `[C, 1.05 × C]`: 5% of C of *additional* ledger bytes above the cap. The band is only a promise if the bytes exist, so the collector requires, continuously, `free_disk_on_volume ≥ (1.05 × C − ledger bytes) + 2 × WAL_24h + fault_reserve`, where `fault_reserve` is 64 MiB kept for gap rows, receipts and the fault marker; while that inequality holds the band is **physically present** and usage admission continues at the cap. When it does not hold (the volume, not the ledger, is the limit), the band is declared exhausted early: the collector stops tailing, declares a gap with `count_basis = 'unknown'` and null counts (`dropped_usage_rows = null`), and `/status` shows `reserve.present = false` with the shortfall. Resume when under 90% of C and the inequality holds again. The watermark carries the gap; the cloud page shows it as a floor or as unknown loss. Never report completeness from a successful HTTP response if items were discarded (`footprint-budgets.md:29`). This is a disclosed product trade-off: a capped host stops capturing activity rather than growing without bound.

**When the volume is full and a gap cannot be written.** The gap row and the cursor advance are one transaction, so a failed write consumes nothing (ARCHITECTURE.md §6.3): the unread bytes stay unread and keep being reported. The collector then sets the in-memory fault, tries the marker file (an unlink needs no space; the write may fail), frees only what it owns and nothing in the never-delete set (`PRAGMA wal_checkpoint(TRUNCATE)`, then `error_samples`, then `metric_samples` older than 1 h), and retries the gap write; capture of activity stops, usage admission continues only while the band is physically present, `/status` shows `storage_full`, and every claim attests nothing (`through = null`, `unattested = 'gap_record_unavailable'`) until the fault row and its gap are durable and acknowledged; a crash in that state is covered by the restart rule (ARCHITECTURE.md §6.3; `out/checks/counterexamples.log`, case 13).

### 4.5 CPU and write rate

The summarizer, projection refresh, receipt compaction and backfill run in one work loop with a token bucket sized from the class figure; capture tailing is not throttled except at the 100% ledger stop in the enforced regime. Backfill is the first thing paused when the 1-minute CPU or write share exceeds the figure. The tailer catch-up (`.163.81` revisit queue) is not throttled by this program; its own bounded queue is the throttle (`LANE-ROOT-CAUSE.md:3`).

### 4.6 RSS

The worker process checkpoints and restarts itself when its RSS exceeds the cap for 5 minutes (the supervisor/worker split already exists: two processes measured, `HOSTS.md:4`). Snapshot and summary batches are bounded (default 5,000 rows) so a single busy session (1.88 M rows on Studio0, `HOSTS.md:19`) is processed in pages, never loaded whole.

## 5. Disclosure

`/status` gains a `budget` block: mode (advisory or enforced, with the stage or the calibration receipt), class (or `unclassified: catch-up pending`), each measure with current value, target, percentage, the reduction steps in effect with timestamps, gaps declared in the last 24 h with their count and interval basis, open faults and the restart state, the tailer-completeness fields, the retention hold with `runway_days`, the ladder rung in effect, `reserve.present`, the conversion throttle's state and pause reasons, and the retention values currently in force (R, usage-fact days). The local dashboard shows a one-line banner past 75% in the enforced regime and whenever the ladder is on a rung. The cloud coverage strip shows declared gaps, faults and unread bytes per machine (ARCHITECTURE.md §6). A weekly line in the collector log records the 24 h p50/p95 of every measure, which is the evidence the acceptance proof reads (PROOF.md §3).

## 6. What would change the numbers

- If B1 shows RSS is dominated by SQLite page cache rather than projection state, the RSS caps are met by `cache_size` rather than by restarting the worker.
