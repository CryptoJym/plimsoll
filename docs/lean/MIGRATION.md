## 1. Invariants that hold on every host at every step

1. **No raw row is deleted by any job between the hold and S9, except under the disclosed hold-release ladder.** From S1b the prune is held (ARCHITECTURE.md §2.4); today's prune would otherwise delete a pending-upload row at age (`collector-cli/src/buffer.ts:3008-3013,3041-3052`). The ladder's rung at fewer than 5 days of runway deletes only rows whose delivery is acknowledged (`uploaded_at` set and an `acknowledged` receipt for the row's own id, `collector-cli/src/outbox.ts:662-671,701-709`), never a member of an open target, and writes a receipt and a tombstone; the S3 certify converts the affected sessions as `cloud_superset` (§3, §6). A rollback through S8 therefore replays or rebuilds from every raw row that is not in the cloud already; **after a rung-2 release** the old reader path is reconstructable only from the rows still present, and the difference is exactly the receipted release set (§8; `out/fixtures/sf1_ladder_rollback_parity.py`).
2. **Growth is bounded, measured continuously, and disclosed, never denied.** Dual-write and conversion add disk, writes and CPU while they run. The new tables are estimated at **about 58.6% of the raw table's bytes** on Studio1 (366 MB against 624 MB, identity rows and edges included; `out/checks/plan-arithmetic.log` "Round 6"; `out/fixtures/s1b_runway_geometry.py`), and during S2-S8 the old tables remain, so a light ledger grows about 27% before any deletion; Studio0's growth is 21-26 GB at that ratio if its raw share is 50-60%, an estimate the copy replaces. The hold's runway (BUDGETS.md §4.2) is recomputed every minute from measured growth for as long as the hold is on, and every stage can be stopped with nothing lost.
3. **Delivery evidence is never deleted.** The never-delete set in ARCHITECTURE.md §2.3 is outside every job, including tombstones.
4. **Two rollback regimes, stated per stage.** Up to and including S8, every stage has a flag and old tables, and rollback needs no data restore; the window in which that is true ends at S9's first proved deletion, and F = 7 days is reserved for it after S8. From S9 onward, recovery is **forward repair**. The rebuild swap (S10) is the only step where "the old file back" is a valid rollback, and only while all writers are paused (§4). W_retire = 14 days after S9 is the wait before old tables are dropped and cloud raw activity rows are deleted; it is not a rollback window. A second forward-repair boundary exists on the cloud: once a tenant's first cloud deletion has run, the activity lane is closed for the installs that passed S7 (ARCHITECTURE.md §5.3), so flipping S7 back after that point re-uploads nothing.
5. **Budgets are advisory until S10 completes on that host** (BUDGETS.md §4.1). No ledger-size cap is enforced on a pre-migration ledger.
6. **Nothing is called captured that a tailer never read, and nothing is called complete while a durability fault or an unverified restart is open.** S3, S6 and S7 depend on the accepted `.163.81` and `.163.82` fixes, zero deferred bytes, a completed whole-root walk, the B22 durability change and no open fault (ARCHITECTURE.md §6.3-6.4).
7. **A sealed segment is terminal.** Nothing after S9 ever rebuilds an aggregate from a partial member set (ARCHITECTURE.md §1 item 7, §2.2).
8. **Studio0 goes last**, after a rehearsal on its own ledger copy on another machine, and nothing heavy runs on Studio0 itself until the rehearsal has measured it (`HOSTS.md:19`).

## 3. Converting existing ledgers

- **What converts**: every raw row becomes either a usage fact (identity and lineage kept, ARCHITECTURE.md §3.1) or a member of a turn/session segment and its day segments and hourly rollup (activity rows). The `<UR>` predicate decides which, never the event type. Every compact segment becomes `activity_day_facts` rows with `origin = 'compact_segment'` keyed by the UTC day of the item's parsed instant (ARCHITECTURE.md §3.5); every surviving orphan fact becomes rows with `origin = 'event_fact'`. Historical sessions are sealed by the same seal rules as live ones (a session whose last activity is more than 30 minutes before the conversion and whose disposition is terminal seals immediately; every historical hourly bucket and day segment seals), each seal with its exhaustive recompute while every member exists.
- **Rows released under the hold ladder**: a session with a `lean_hold_release_acked` receipt for one of its ids is converted from its remaining raw rows, flagged `released_members = n`, and its collector-origin segment is expected to relate as `cloud_superset` to the cloud (the cloud holds the acknowledged rows); the reader uses cloud-origin for it (§6). The same rule covers sessions whose rows expired before the lean writer existed.
- **Order inside a host**: newest sessions first for the live path (S2) so the dashboard is correct immediately; oldest first for the backfill (S3) so a later deletion frees the oldest pages.
- **Conversion throttle (its own, measured; BUDGETS.md §4.2)**: the converter is the lowest-priority consumer of the work loop. It pauses when the 1-minute collector CPU exceeds 0.5 core on a light host or 2 cores on Studio0 scale, when WAL bytes written exceed the class write hypothesis for 5 consecutive minutes, when free disk on the volume falls under the reserve, or when the runway falls under 10 days. It never pauses on ledger size, because conversion grows the file by design and a pre-migration ledger is above every cap. Its progress, pauses and reasons are in `/status`.
- **The day-key and boundary correction is enumerated.** The S0 shape census gives, per host, the rows in each of the three census classes of ARCHITECTURE.md §3.5; on Studio3 all three counts are zero (202,074 rows, all canonical, `out/checks/ledger-shape-census.log`). S3 records for every host the number of census rows whose day moved and, per window cutoff, the rows whose membership flipped, and the certify (c) requires the schema-2 versus schema-3 difference to equal exactly that set (PROOF.md §4).
- **Studio0 baseline (blocker 3).** Studio0's published snapshots are stale: `publishSnapshots` refuses while any repair backlog exists (`collector-cli/src/dashboard-projection.ts:3635-3641`) and 31.4 M pending revisions have been queued since 08-20 (`HOSTS.md:19`). So the S0 baseline on Studio0 is **not** the published snapshot. It is the raw-row and compact-segment **oracle** on the copy (an independent SQL implementation of each window's groups with the day-aligned cutoff and the UTC day key, written by B9a and itself tested against the schema-2 snapshot on Studio5 and Studio1 where the projection is converged, with the census correction enumerated), plus, if the `.163.84` release candidate converges the projection on the copy within the rehearsal, the converged schema-2 snapshot as a second reference. The certify on Studio0 compares the schema-3 snapshot with the oracle; equality with a stale published snapshot is not demanded.
- **Studio0 specifics** (from `HOSTS.md:19`, all to be measured on the copy first): 100,000 learning facts at the cap with 292,934 refused mean the failure/retry counters come from the summarizer's own correlation, not from `tool_attempt_facts`. The busiest session has 1.88 M rows; its segments are folded in pages, sealed every 7 days of segment age, its turn series is bounded (ARCHITECTURE.md §3.3) and its id exchange may need about 380 pages (§5.3). A large share of the 73 GB may be rows protected from deletion because their delivery never terminated; the first query on the copy is the acknowledged/dead/pending split by month, because rows in the never-delete set cannot be removed by any stage here and cap the saving. Receipts and root observations are kept by this program (B14 deferred) and are estimated at about 9% of the ledger if Studio0's mix matches Studio1's (`out/checks/plan-arithmetic.log`); tombstones are sized from its measured activity ratio; the rehearsal measures both. The rehearsal also measures **peak growth during S2-S3, peak WAL and the gross growth per day**, which become Studio0's S1b numbers. The old upgrade snapshots beside the ledger have already been pruned (three remain); the rehearsal's free-space calculation uses a fresh `df` on Studio0. Studio0's unread session files were not read by the capture-gaps check (`UNCOVERED-EVIDENCE.txt:2`); the read-only query is run there first and the catch-up is sized before S1b.

## 4. Physical space reclaim under a full writer pause (auto_vacuum is off)

`PRAGMA auto_vacuum = 0` on the measured ledger (`VALUE-MAP.md:35`), so deletes free pages for reuse but never shrink the file. Reclaim is a maintenance rebuild with every SQLite writer stopped, not only the file tailers: thirty-one collector modules write the ledger (`out/checks/writer-modules.log`, a static statement inventory; that every live writer holds a lease is proved by the drills), and hooks, OTLP and live usage arrive by push (`collector-cli/src/capture-frontier.ts:43-44`; spools in `capture-spool-state.ts`).

1. **Preconditions**: S10 reached (or an abort under step 8); outbox pending counted and carried (not required to be zero); free disk ≥ live bytes × 1.2 + the WAL high-water of the last 24 h (live bytes = (page_count − freelist_count) × page_size); `PRAGMA integrity_check` ok on the live file.
2. **Quiesce**: the supervisor puts the daemon into `maintenance.rebuild`: stop the tailers and the maintenance loop; stop session-sync and outbox uploads; stop the dashboard projection and the learning materializer; the hook and OTLP HTTP endpoints answer 503 with `Retry-After` so clients spool; every writer module holds a named lease in `maintenance_state` and the rebuild proceeds only when every lease is released, then the worker process exits so exactly one connection remains. The pause is declared in `/status` and in the next capture claim as `unattested = 'maintenance_rebuild'` with `through = null` until the spools drain after resume; it is a pause, not a gap, because nothing is lost.
3. **Checkpoint and copy**: `PRAGMA wal_checkpoint(TRUNCATE)`; confirm the WAL is empty; `VACUUM INTO '<ledger>.rebuild'`. `VACUUM INTO` writes only live pages and needs headroom equal to the live size, not 2× the old file.
4. **Verify the rebuild before the swap**: `PRAGMA integrity_check`; row counts of every retained table equal (tombstones included); the five snapshots regenerated from the rebuild equal the live file's; finance publication equal; `PRAGMA user_version` and schema hash equal.
5. **Swap**: rename the live file to `<ledger>.pre-lean-<date>`, rename the rebuild into place, reopen, run `integrity_check` again, resume writers in reverse order, drain the spools.
6. **Rollback boundary**: if any check in step 4 fails, delete the rebuild and resume on the untouched live file. If the reopen in step 5 fails, rename `.pre-lean-<date>` back before any writer resumes; nothing was written in between because every writer was paused, so the old file is exactly current. **Once a writer has resumed, the old file is a frozen snapshot, not a rollback target**: swapping it back would lose new raw rows, cursor state, receipts and outbox state (`collector-cli/src/buffer.ts:2401-2416`; `collector-cli/src/outbox.ts:678-709`). It is kept for W_retire as forensic evidence only, and `/status` says so.
7. **Drills on a copy before any live rebuild** (PROOF.md §6): kill −9 during `VACUUM INTO`; a non-empty WAL at checkpoint time; free-space refusal at the precondition; a forced reopen failure with the rename-back path; a client pushing hooks during the pause and the spool draining afterwards with the claim recovering.
8. **Abort recovery after S2 or S3, and the ladder's abort rung**: `lean.write=off`, drop the new tables, then run steps 1-6 on the host; the file returns to at most its pre-S2 live bytes **plus the raw capture since S2** (raw rows admitted since S2, counted by B1, × the host's measured bytes per raw row), because the hold deleted nothing while capture continued (`out/fixtures/sf_abort_rebuild_bound.py`). Because dropping tables returns no bytes on its own, the tool and its drills are accepted **before S2** (S1b (c)) on a copy that has been through a copy-ready S2/S3 (B2a), and the runway subtracts the rebuild's own headroom so the abort path is always executable.
9. **If disk headroom is insufficient**: no rebuild; growth still stops because freed pages are reused; `/status` says "N GB reclaimable; needs M GB free".
10. **Alternative for very large files**: an incremental copy (`sqlite3_backup` pages per step) into a new file while capture continues, then a final short catch-up under the same full pause. Chosen by the rehearsal if the plain rebuild pause on the Studio0 copy exceeds 15 minutes. The same rollback boundary applies to its final swap.

**Cloud reclaim**: after S7 for every active host and after the cloud deletion in §6, dead space in `ai_interaction_events` (17 GB heap, `PROD-FACTS.md:8`) is reused by new rows; a physical shrink needs `pg_repack` or a `VACUUM (FULL)` window, decided by the lead against Supabase's plan; it is not required for correctness or read speed because the readers use the partial usage index (`DESIGN.md §4.7`).

## 6. Cloud history

- Existing activity rows in `ai_interaction_events` (about 83% of 13.2 M rows by the one-hour mix, `PROD-FACTS.md:8,17-29`) are converted by a bounded, resumable backfill in the lane-2 house style (`begin`, `backfill`, `certify`, `DESIGN.md §4.7`), walking `(session_id, id)` in chunks under the CLI's lifted timeout, never on the request path, into **cloud-origin** summaries, segments, day rows and rollups with their own membership counts and additive digests over event ids; a cloud-origin segment's boundary is the cloud's own `created_at` at backfill (exact because it is the cloud's column), and its per-actor parts come from the raw rows' `actor_id` (ARCHITECTURE.md §5.2-5.3).
- **Origins are kept apart and related exactly** (ARCHITECTURE.md §5.3). The reader rule is the stored relation, never a default preference: `equal` and `collector_superset` read collector-origin, `cloud_superset` reads cloud-origin (this now includes sessions whose rows were released under the hold ladder), `partial` and `unknown` read neither and display flagged. The certify report lists every `partial` session with both digests and counts; a disagreement is never resolved by overwriting either.
- The existing `ai_work_sessions` sync record stays as it is (grow-only totals, `cloud src/lib/session-sync.ts:156-160,192-210`); the new tables are siblings (ARCHITECTURE.md §10).
- **Certify (exhaustive)**: as in S4. Additionally, export for ≥ 5 test actors equal before and after the reader switch; on a fixture with A→B→C and A→B→A bindings, each actor's export holds exactly its parts and B's part is present; tenant erasure on a disposable cluster leaves zero residue across the new tables.
- **Delete**: only after certify, after every cloud reader has been on the new path for W_retire, and after S7 on every active host. Chunks are per sealed segment, ≤ 5,000 rows, `SET LOCAL statement_timeout` under the 25 s runtime ceiling (`DESIGN.md §2`; `cloud src/lib/route-budget.ts:71-77`), selected by `segment_ref`, and the delete statement re-asserts inside its transaction the **residue proof** of ARCHITECTURE.md §5.3: the relation is `equal`, `collector_superset` or `cloud_superset`, the segment is sealed, the remaining assigned rows equal `member_count − deleted_member_count − |lacking ids|`, and (at the first chunk) the recomputed additive digest equals the stored one; otherwise it deletes nothing and marks the segment `audit_failed`. A second and every later chunk passes on the residue, never on the original count (`out/checks/counterexamples.log`, case 4). After the tenant's first deletion the activity lane is closed for every install that passed S7 (invariant 4). Usage rows are never deleted by this program.

## 7. Parallel run and parity against today's numbers

Every gate compares the old path and the new path at the same frozen instant on the same host, so live capture cannot explain a difference; for the local dashboard the instant is UTC midnight so the millisecond and day-aligned cutoffs coincide, and the census rows are the only permitted difference, enumerated (ARCHITECTURE.md §3.5). The full metric-by-metric list, tolerances and evidence files are in PROOF.md. Two rules: a difference in a **count** fails the stage unless it is exactly the census correction; a difference that is only **more coverage** on the new path (failure counters beyond the learning cap; day-grain history beyond raw retention) passes and is recorded with its reason. Sampling never substitutes for the exhaustive certify steps in S3, S4 and S9.
